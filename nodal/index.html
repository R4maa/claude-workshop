The amber dot is where the plate is being shaken; drag it. The strip shows how loudly the plate rings at each pitch from that spot: peaks are resonances, and each one draws a different figure. Between peaks the plate barely moves and the sand only creeps. The mode shapes are the textbook cosine approximation for a free square plate, so real plates draw rounder cousins of these.

Nodal

In 1787 Ernst Chladni drew a violin bow down the edge of a metal plate strewn with sand. The sand jumped away from wherever the plate shook and settled along the lines where it stayed still.

This plate hums one steady pitch. Slide the pitch, or move the spot where it is shaken, and the sand redraws itself.
