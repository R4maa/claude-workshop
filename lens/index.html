Lens

A black hole that does not spin, with a thin disc of hot gas around it. For every pixel, the page follows one ray of light backwards from your eye through curved space until it falls in, hits the disc, or escapes to the stars.

What to look for. The far side of the disc appears lifted over the top of the shadow, and its underside is bent into view below. Hugging the shadow is a thin ring of light that circled the hole before leaving. The side of the disc turning toward you is brighter and bluer. Stars near the edge slide and stretch as you move. Switch Gravity off to see the same scene with light travelling straight.

What is exact. The light paths, the size of the shadow (2.6 times the hole's radius, seen from afar) and the disc's colour shift for a distant viewer. Distances are counted in radii of the hole itself.

What is not. The disc's swirling texture is decoration, its brightness is tone-mapped to fit a screen, stars are not brightened where lensing would magnify them, and real black holes spin.
